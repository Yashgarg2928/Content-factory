# 🎨 Style Guide: Line Art – Black & White  

**Step 1 – Core Rendering Style**  
- Minimalistic 2D line drawing.  
- Clean thin black outlines only.  
- No shading, no colors.  
- Plain white background.  
- Flat orthographic perspective.  
- Professional and symbolic tone (not cartoonish).  
- HEX codes from characters are used as palette references only (not rendered).  

**Step 2 – Composition Rules**  
- Environments and characters should be drawn simply, symbolic rather than realistic.  
- Camera angles in the meta-prompt (low-angle, wide, etc.) are interpreted as **flat layout positioning** (e.g., left, right, centered).  
- Lens and depth-of-field details are ignored.  

**Step 3 – Lighting & Mood**  
- No realistic lighting or shadows.  
- Emotional tone is conveyed through **expressions, poses, and symbolic background elements**.  

**Step 4 – Final Negative Prompts**  
--no extra limbs, --no character duplication, --no face blending, --no distortions, --no gradients, --no textures, --no 3D, --no color

# 🎨 Style Guide: Ultra-Realistic Selfie-Style (POV)  

**Step 1 – Core Rendering Style**  
- Ultra-realistic rendering with cinematic immersion.  
- Selfie perspective: one arm partially in frame, subject looking directly into the lens.  
- Visible skin details (texture, pores, sweat, freckles as appropriate).  
- Realistic fabric details (wrinkles, folds, textures).  
- Shallow depth of field with soft bokeh.  
- Cinematic lighting (natural, environmental, or dramatic depending on the scene).  
- HEX codes from characters are rendered as real textures and colors.  

**Step 2 – Composition Rules**  
- Always frame as if the subject is taking the photo themselves.  
- The extended arm should appear partially in-frame.  
- Eye contact with the “camera” is mandatory.  
- Use close-up or medium-close framing, wide lenses (24–35mm) for selfie realism.  

**Step 3 – Lighting & Mood**  
- Naturalistic lighting (sunlight, indoor warm glow, moonlight, etc.).  
- Atmosphere enhanced by environmental effects (dust, fog, particles, sun flares).  
- Emotions expressed through subtle realism (eyes, posture, facial tension).  

**Step 4 – Final Negative Prompts**

# 🎨 Style Guide: Retro Pixel Art Game Style  

**Step 1 – Core Rendering Style**  
- Retro pixel art inspired by 8-bit and 16-bit video games (NES, SNES, Sega Genesis).  
- Visible pixel grid, blocky proportions, simplified anatomy.  
- Limited color palette (~8–32 colors only).  
- Pixel shading with dithering, no gradients.  
- Bold outlines and sharp contrast.  
- 2D side view or isometric perspective.  
- Nostalgic, dramatic, arcade-inspired tone.  
- HEX codes from characters are converted into simplified pixel palette approximations.  

**Step 2 – Composition Rules**  
- Camera angles from the meta-prompt are interpreted as **pixelated perspectives**:  
  - “Wide shot” = side-scrolling stage.  
  - “Close-up” = larger sprite focus.  
  - “Low angle” = sprite slightly scaled from below.  
- Ignore realistic lens/focal lengths.  
- Keep backgrounds simple but atmospheric (pixelated sunset, ruins, explosions).  

**Step 3 – Lighting & Mood**  
- Use contrast in pixel shading (bright vs dark) to convey mood.  
- Atmosphere comes from pixel-level details (burning sunset, glowing pixel explosions, flickering lights).  
- Tone can be dramatic, heroic, or nostalgic.  

**Step 4 – Final Negative Prompts**  
--no extra limbs, --no character duplication, --no face blending, --no distortions, --no gradients, --no textures, --no photorealism, --no 3D renders, --no anti-aliasing, --no smooth shading

# 🎨 Style Guide: Ultra-Realistic Split-Image Contrast  

**Step 1 – Core Rendering Style**  
- Ultra-realistic split-image composition.  
- Frame divided visually into left and right halves.  
- Each half shows a contrasting subject, environment, or lifestyle.  
- Natural skin texture and realistic anatomy (no over-polished or plasticky look).  
- Real-world lighting and cinematic color grading.  
- Depth of field for focus and storytelling.  
- HEX codes from characters are rendered as real-world colors and textures.  

**Step 2 – Composition Rules**  
- The frame must be clearly split:  
  - Left = Subject A with unique environment, mood, and props.  
  - Right = Subject B with contrasting environment, mood, and props.  
- Camera angles from the meta-prompt should balance across both halves (e.g., eye-level split shot).  
- Lighting should emphasize contrast (bright vs moody, warm vs cold).  

**Step 3 – Lighting & Mood**  
- Cinematic lighting with strong contrasts between the two halves.  
- Use environmental storytelling to emphasize differences (e.g., order vs chaos, rich vs poor).  
- Maintain realistic textures (clothing folds, skin pores, material detail).  

**Step 4 – Final Negative Prompts**
